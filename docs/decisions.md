# Decisions

Every call the port makes that the original didn't, numbered, with the
reason. The original is the specification; nothing changes quietly.
Decisions 1-8 are the brief's D1-D8 (`docs/brief.md`, section 3), which
Matt took as recommended on 2026-10-18.

| # | Date | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 2026-10-18 | **The Atari ST version is the specification**: Argonaut's own `STAR.PRG`, run in Hatari as the oracle. **Exact where the game decides, approximate where it only draws**: the game's logic (the 16-bit world, the handlers, speeds, timers, random numbers, collision boxes, scores, levels) is transcribed exactly, with 16- and 32-bit arithmetic where the ST uses it, so the object table can be checked against Hatari's byte for byte. The rendering is the BBC's own (byte vertices, matrices and projection at the precision the screen needs), checked against the ST's projected lines to within a pixel. Where the logic reads the rendering (the camera-space positions the collisions and the scanner use, the laser's test on projected boxes), those are computed at the ST's precision | It's the game the blessing is for, and the complete one: all 28 models and their animations, the speech and the song. The 8-bit versions are other studios' remakes with their own models, and each ran at about 6 fps by doing its maths the slow way (docs/brief.md, section 1). A 68000 program can't be transcribed instruction by instruction onto a 6502, so the line is drawn where it can be checked: logic by state, drawing by lines. |
| 2 | 2026-10-18 | **The wireframe is the ST's**: each object's back faces culled, one colour an object, every line ORed, objects see-through to one another. No filled polygons, no hidden-line removal between objects | It is the original's look, and the manual's: the AGAV's "Enhanced Vision System" gives "a stunning translucent display of anything within its visual range, even if objects are behind solid buildings". Every version drew it this way. ORed lines need no depth sort and combine where two colours cross, as the ST's bitplanes do; EOR would leave a black pixel where two lines of one colour cross. |
| 3 | 2026-10-18 | **The screen is MODE 1 at the ST's 320 x 200**, double-buffered with both screens at the same address in main and shadow RAM: draw with ACCCON's X, show with D, flip both at VSync. The view keeps the ST's window, projection centre and panel coordinates as they are. The view's three line colours are still to choose, from rendered pictures; the cockpit takes its own four from a palette split at the view's bottom edge | The ST's low res and MODE 1 clock 320 pixels a line at 8MHz with one scan line a pixel row, so the two machines' pixels are the same size and the ST's coordinates carry over unchanged. MODE 2 would halve the detail across; MODE 4 has one colour; the CPC's 256 x 192 cuts 64 pixels from the view. The same address in both banks means no drawing routine is patched per buffer (docs/research/beeb.md, section 2). |
| 4 | 2026-10-18 | **The game steps at a fixed rate, the ST's mean in flight**, and frames are dropped when the scene is heavy: four VBLs a step (12.5 a second), or a VIA timer at the ST's measured mean, to be settled by the speed spike. Whether docking's tunnel keeps the ST's faster pace is for later | The ST steps its game once per rendered frame, so it plays faster when there is less to draw: 25 fps docked, 12.5-13.5 in flight, 9 near the towers [measured 2026-10-17, docs/research/st.md]. A slower Master would slow the whole game under that rule. Port the decision (the game was tuned at its usual pace), not the accident (busy scenes slowed everything), as nm's decision 12 did. |
| 5 | 2026-10-18 | **Sound**: the ST's 24 YM effects transcribed to the SN76489; **Clare Edgeley's four speech clips sampled at 6kHz**, or 4kHz if the frame rate can't bear it, played from a User VIA timer without stopping the game. **The title song is chosen by ear**: the ST's sample (loaded for the title only, or streamed from disc) heard beside David Lowe's AY arrangement from the Spectrum 128K converted to the SN | The ST plays its speech without stopping; the Spectrum 128K stopped for each phrase [measured, docs/research/zx.md]. Speech costs about a third of the CPU at 6kHz while a clip plays (docs/research/beeb.md, section 5). The 18-second sampled song doesn't fit beside the game, so its options trade memory or disc time against the vocal (docs/brief.md, D5). |
| 6 | 2026-10-18 | **Controls: the ST's keys and the analogue joystick**, the joystick proportional as the ST's mouse is. An AMX mouse is for later, if wanted | The ST banks and climbs in proportion to the mouse; the BBC's analogue port is the nearest thing on every Master. |
| 7 | 2026-10-18 | **A Master 128 only, on one disc**: the code uses the 65C12's instructions, shadow RAM and the four sideways RAM banks. The drawing code and its tables live in low RAM and HAZEL (never paged); the shapes, the game logic and the speech in sideways banks. A Model B edition would be a separate project | Two 16,000-byte screens leave about 95K for everything else, of which about 23K is never paged (docs/research/beeb.md, section 2). The ST's game fits there, but not on a B. |
| 8 | 2026-10-18 | **No co-processor edition** (Matt, 2026-10-18, after the step back's plan, `docs/plan.md`). As first taken: the design keeps a seam between the game and the drawing (logic and transform on one side, a list of lines on the other) so that a 6502 co-processor edition (Elite's split, on the Master's internal Turbo) stays possible, to be decided once the single-processor version runs. A VideoNuLA edition (the ST's exact colours) is still for later | The game is made for the plain Master 128, and the seam is no longer a requirement. The co-processor would have paid, as the maths is most of the frame (`docs/plan.md`, section 1), but it isn't what the port is for. |
| 9 | 2026-10-18 | **The shapes are kept as bytes, and each face's visibility is tested in object space.** A vertex is three signed bytes k, the ST's coordinate divided by the shape's gcd (every shape's coordinates share one, and with it every k fits a byte); the energy beam's coordinates (multiples of 128 up to 32,000) are rounded to multiples of 256. Vertices no frame moves, and faces whose normal no frame changes, are kept once for the shape; a frame named twice by an animation list is kept once; shape 10 is shape 1's data, as on the ST (`tools/mkshapes.py`). Each face carries its normal (the ST's (v1 - v0) x (v2 - v0), scaled to bytes) and K, the normal's dot product with vertex 2: the face is visible when the camera, in the object's space, is on its outer side. The ST transforms three vertices a face and tests their cross product in camera space | The ST's 34,328 bytes of shapes don't fit a 16K bank as they are; as bytes they take one bank. Testing in object space costs three products a face and transforms only the vertices that are drawn, where the ST's test transformed three times as many vertices as it drew (docs/research/st.md, "Work per frame"). On the 693 frames captured (flight, the towers, the silo tunnel), the two tests choose the same edges for all but 2% of them, nearly all on objects more than 1,500 units away, where the ST's own test works on vertices rounded to 4 units; every edge both draw is the same to the pixel, the beam's within one (`make spike`, 2026-10-18). |
| 10 | 2026-10-18 | **An object 16 or more of its radii away is drawn with its perspective made linear across it**: its centre is projected, and its vertices go straight onto the screen by a first-order matrix (two rows, six products a vertex, no division), its edges clipped to the window only when the object reaches past it. Nearer objects are transformed into camera space and each vertex projected, by a reciprocal of the depth's 9-bit mantissa (exact below 512, where the mantissa is), with the ST's divs overflow kept where it happens | It's the drawing only (decision 1). The ST divides every projected vertex by its own depth, which on the Master is the dearest part of a vertex. Across an object 16 radii away, the second-order term of the perspective is under half a pixel, and 73% of the objects drawn in flight are that far [measured 2026-10-18, the flight capture]. With it the flight capture's objects take 170K cycles a frame, not 186K; every edge both draw is within a pixel of the ST's but for a handful clipped at the window's edge, where a nearly level line's end can slide further than the line moves (`make spike`, 2026-10-18). |
| 11 | 2026-10-18 | **Objects the game can't be deciding about are rotated into camera space approximately.** Every object on the coarse list is first rotated from the high bytes of its offset (within 444 units of the ST's result); only one that could then be in the view's pyramid, or within 2,000 units of the player on every axis (the largest collision box), is rotated exactly, as the ST does it. The others keep the approximation in their camera-space position, which only the scanner reads for them, and never reach the draw list. The checks compare camera-space positions exactly for the objects rotated exactly, and for the rest check that the ST's own result is within the bound and neither drawn nor near (`tools/scenecheck.py`) | The ST's coarse test keeps the whole band within &2020 of the player in z whatever x is, so about 40 objects are rotated every frame where 4 or 5 are drawn (docs/research/st.md); exact, each costs the Master up to 2,700 cycles when the player banks or climbs. What reads the camera-space positions is the drawing, the laser and the mines (drawn objects only), the collisions and refuelling (within their boxes), and the scanner (drawing). In the 693 frames captured, about 6 objects a frame need the exact rotation; the flight's list phase falls from 65K to 54K cycles a frame on average and its worst from 113K to 81K (`make spike`, 2026-10-18). The scanner's dots, at 1/512 scale, move by under a pixel. |
| 12 | 2026-10-18 | **The disc stays in use after loading, read by the game's own 1770 reader**, as Chuckie Egg 2's streaming edition reads it (an NMI routine at &0D00, reads in the background while the game runs, no OS call after start-up). **The front end is loaded from disc when it's wanted, and so is speech, or it's streamed** (Matt, 2026-10-18); what else is loaded by phase, as Elite loads its docked and flying code (the docked screens at docking, the level layouts at a level's start, the cockpit picture), is for the memory map (`docs/plan.md`). Speech stays at 6kHz if the memory map allows, 4kHz if not (decision 5) | The game as the ST has it wants about 117K on the Master, which has about 96K (`docs/plan.md`, section 2); what play doesn't need at a given moment can come from disc. CE2S measured the reader on a Master's 1770: a sector in 20ms warm, a track a revolution, 24% of the CPU while a sector reads, 1.3s from cold as the motor spins up, and the drive left to stop when idle (CE2's decision 35: kept running, it annoyed Matt) [measured, `../chuckie-egg-2-beeb/docs/streaming.md`]. |
| 13 | 2026-10-18 | **The object table keeps only the fields the port uses**, not the ST's 64-byte records (Matt, 2026-10-18). The checks against the ST translate between the two layouts | The ST's 142 records take 9.1K; the plan reckons the fields the port uses at about half of that (`docs/plan.md`, section 2), to be counted as the logic is transcribed. Memory is what's short, and translating layouts is the checker's job, done once. |
| 14 | 2026-10-18 | **The frame loop** (`src/engine.6502`). A step is the list phase then the game's logic, so the lists are made every step, not every frame; the last step before a frame draws it between the two. At most four steps run before each frame: past 16 fields a frame, the game slows. Each frame is drawn into the screen not shown and shown from the next vertical sync by the interrupt; each screen keeps a list of the lines drawn on it, in shadow RAM below the shadow screen, which are drawn again as zeros before it's drawn next, and if the list overflows the screen is cleared whole | The ST's logic reads the lists the renderer made (the collisions and refuelling on the near list's camera-space positions, the handlers' near-list tests, the laser and mines on the draw list) [measured, docs/research/st.md, "The frame"], so for the logic to be exact a step must make them as an ST frame does. The ST draws between its lists and its logic, so a frame drawn there shows what the ST drew at that step. The heaviest captured scene takes about 11 fields to draw; the cap only stops a pathological frame from running the game on unseen. Erasing lines costs what drawing them did, less than clearing the view, which the ST does every frame, but for the silo tunnel's long lines (`make spike`, 2026-10-18). The list phase is now 30% of the CPU at 12.5 steps a second (`docs/plan.md`, section 1), which makes it the optimisation pass's first target. |
| 15 | 2026-10-18 | **The cockpit's colours** (decision 3's open part; option b, Matt, 2026-10-18). Above the palette split the view keeps black, red, cyan and white; below it the panel is black, red, cyan and blue: one logical colour changes at the split (`tools/mkcockpit.py`) | The ST's panel has fourteen colours, so every choice loses some. Keeping red keeps the gauges' bars and lamps, which the player reads at a glance, at the cost of the panel's few white highlights; a third band for the frame's yellow lamps (option c) would cost a second palette split every frame. |
| 16 | 2026-10-18 | **What the logic draws comes after the step's logic, onto the same frame** (option B, Matt, 2026-10-18): the enemies' laser lines, the mines' messages and the player's bolt are drawn after the last step's logic, before that frame is shown. This changes decision 14's step: the frame is flagged ready after its logic, not straight after its drawing | On the ST the handlers draw into the frame being drawn, after world_render's draw loop. Recording the shots and drawing them on the next frame (option A) would show each one a step late, 80 ms at 12.5 steps a second, a laser line trailing its attacker in a turn. Showing the frame after the logic makes a heavy step a little likelier to miss a field. |
| 17 | 2026-10-18 | **The cheat prompt and Interrogate run as loops inside the step**, as on the ST (option b, Matt, 2026-10-18), drawing and flipping frames through the engine's own routines; the engine discards the fields counted while they ran, so no catch-up steps follow, and counts fields in 16 bits for the cheat's 1,501-field timeouts | It keeps the ST's structure (the prompt blocks inside read_keys, in effect a pause; Interrogate runs its own frames there), so the transcription stays checkable against it. Engine states (option a) would split read_keys part-way. |
| 18 | 2026-10-18 | **The missile view's lock-up at a level change is kept** (option a, Matt, 2026-10-18): a missile launched in the frame where world_render raised the level leaves the change pending at every frame's end, the view never ending, as on the ST | It is the original's, decision 1 makes the logic exact, and the window is one frame in each 10,000 points; clearing the flag on the missile path too (option b) would be one store, a deviation recorded here if it's ever wanted. Measured on the ST: 89 level changes in a row over 88 frames, until Esc. |
| 19 | 2026-10-18 | **A ram costs level 15's 7,500 from level 16 on** (option a, Matt, 2026-10-18), where the ST reads past its 16-entry table | The designed table levels off at 7,500 from level 3 to 15, so carrying on keeps the designers' figure. What the ST reads past it depends on the machine: in Hatari 37,230 for levels 16-30, 224 at 31 and a bus error that stops the game from 32 (docs/research/st-logic.md). |
| 20 | 2026-10-18 | **A small object may be erased by clearing its screen box** rather than drawing its lines again as zeros (a change to decision 14; Matt, 2026-10-18), whichever is cheaper per object, kept only if the measured erase falls as modelled and the erase check stays clean | Everything in the box is the old frame's. The model gave line replay falling from 6.7K to 0.8K cycles a frame in flight and 17.5K to 3.4K at the towers (2026-10-18), against a frame of about 142K. |
| 21 | 2026-10-18 | **Objects rotated approximately get no y'** (a change to decision 11; Matt, 2026-10-18): only x' and z' are approximated, and the checks stop comparing y' for those objects. Built 2026-10-18, after checking the ST's eight reads of an object's y': the draw loops, enemy fire and the player's laser read only the draw list's objects, always rotated exactly; the scanners load y' and overwrite it unused; the collisions test z and x first, which an approximated object always fails | Only the scanner reads their camera-space positions, and it reads x' and z'. About two products an object: estimated at 4K cycles a step banked, 2K a frame on average in the flight capture (2026-10-18). |
| 22 | 2026-10-18 | **Two precision trims in the projection are tried** (within decision 10; Matt, 2026-10-18): far objects' first-order matrix from 8-bit products, and proj_x without its lowest partial product when the shift is 17 or more, each kept only if the scene check's line comparison holds. Built 2026-10-18: the first is kept (B from R's high byte, rounded); the second failed the silo tunnel, whose long level lines cross the window's bottom edge, where a pixel's change slid a clipped end 16 pixels, so it is left out | A far object is at most about 16 pixels across, so 8 bits of B are enough; each trim may move a vertex by a pixel where a value sits on a boundary. Estimated at about 2.4K and 0.6-1.2K cycles a frame in flight (2026-10-18). |
| 23 | 2026-10-18 | **The scanner's dots take their objects' line colours** (Matt, 2026-10-18): red, cyan, or blue where the view draws the object white | The ST's scanner has ten colours and the panel four (decision 15). A dot that matches its object's lines reads at once, and the white things (tanks, homing missiles, launchers) become the only blue dots. The nearest colour would make them cyan, like walkers and towers. |
| 24 | 2026-10-18 | **Colour 0's flashes cover the whole screen, as on the ST, written at the flip** (Matt, 2026-10-18): each ST flash is the nearest of the BBC's colours, written by the interrupt as the frame it belongs to is shown | The ST writes colour 0 at its frame's end after waiting for the raster, and its whole background takes it, the panel's too; docked it is blue on every frame. The panel's blue details merge into it while docked, which matters less than the ST's look. |
| 25 | 2026-10-18 | **The waveforms and the energy bar's dimming advance once per step** (Matt, 2026-10-18), not once per frame drawn | The ST advances them once per frame, and its frame was its step: porting the decision keeps their speed when the port draws fewer frames than it steps. |
| 26 | 2026-10-18 | **A text that goes away leaves its last frame's lines short under its glyphs** (Matt, 2026-10-18): its pixels are cleared after that frame's lines are drawn | For one frame, under text that is disappearing. Making the removal exact costs a third engine call, about 6K cycles a frame while LEVEL shows and 15K while the docked menu does. |
| 27 | 2026-10-18 | **The joystick steers as the ST's mouse does** (Matt, 2026-10-18): each step adds the stick's move into the mouse's accumulators, its travel to either end the turn's and the climb's clamps, the first button the left mouse button, the second the right. The keyboard is the controls that matter: Matt has no joystick | The logic reads the accumulators as the ST's mouse leaves them, so this is the ST's own control model. Its cost is drift when the keys have pushed an accumulator to its clamp. |
| 28 | 2026-10-18 | **The ST's keys the BBC lacks: COPY for Insert, keypad RETURN for keypad Enter, DELETE for Backspace** (Matt, 2026-10-18) | COPY sits by the cursor keys, as Insert does on the ST; the Master has its own keypad RETURN; DELETE is the BBC's backspace. The logic never reads Help or Undo. |
| 29 | 2026-10-18 | **The dots may be erased from a list of their own, and a far dot's height may come from the ground plane** (Matt, 2026-10-18), each kept only if every dot stays within a pixel of the ST's (`make view`). Built 2026-10-18: the dots' own erase list is kept; the ground plane's height was built and measured to break even (the dots the drawing rotates itself had become few, and making the plane whenever the roll or height changes took back its saving), so it is left out | The dots cost about 66K cycles a frame in flight to draw, the largest cost on the screen. Their own list takes 2 bytes a dot where decision 14's line entry takes 6, and the ground plane saves a rotation row for dots far enough not to need it: estimated at about 5K and 5-7K cycles a frame. Which dots are in view stays the logic's exact decision. |
| 30 | 2026-10-18 | **A frame draws every enemy shot fired since the last frame**, each where its own step put it (Matt, 2026-10-18), not only its last step's | The ST draws every step, so every shot is seen. With frames dropped, the last step's alone left more than half the laser lines unseen at 5 frames a second, though their damage, flash and sound happen. |
| 31 | 2026-10-18 | **The checks call an approximated object near by decision 11's own box, 2,000** (the largest collision box), and the port tests its approximation against 2,000 plus the slack (Claude, on Matt's delegation until he can play) | The checks had used 2,512, stricter than decision 11 guarantees, and with the port testing against the same 2,512 any approximation error at that edge could fail them; widening the port's test to 3,024 to stay safe rotated 0.3 to 0.4 more objects a step exactly. Checking what the decision says lets the port test at 2,512: about 420 cycles a frame in flight and 620 a step in a hard turn (estimated 2026-10-18). |
| 32 | 2026-10-18 | **The relevance slack is 444**, not 512 (Claude, on Matt's delegation) | The approximation is within 418 of the ST's result since the turn's rebuild (`tools/mkrotate.py` derives the bound), so 444 still covers it, and fewer objects need rotating exactly. |
| 33 | 2026-10-18 | **An object relevant at the last step is rotated exactly at once**, without its approximation first (a change to decision 11's rule; Claude, on Matt's delegation) | It only ever rotates more objects exactly, never fewer, so no check loosens. In a hard turn about 9.8 approximations a step are skipped for 0.28 needless exact rotations: about 2.1K cycles a step (estimated 2026-10-18). Skipping x' for objects outside the scanner's square was not taken: it would save 0.7-1.5K a step but stop the checks comparing it. |
